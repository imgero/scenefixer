Source: a PostHog export (390 events, 2026-09-19 → 2026-09-25), the user's
Firestore documents, and the uploaded video itself.

## What happened

A user from Santiago, Chile (Windows / Edge, `es-419`, arrived from
chatgpt.com) signed up on 2026-09-24 and uploaded the same 30 s video three
times (`NEKROS CELEBRA .mp4`, 72 MB, 1920x1080, identical md5 across all
three jobs: `fm131mbaag8x6wz3zzeogoru`, `rzcp490vth2mzhwzzwnzirb2`,
`dckfl1gwo29p2lflwswvg91b`). Between the second and third upload they bought
the $10 pack.

They saw the site through **Edge's built-in translator**: every autocaptured
element carries `_msthash`, and the two React #418 hydration errors come
from that. The "EN" button in the event stream is their avatar menu, not a
language toggle.

**The payment was handled correctly.** Pack "10" = $10 = 18 credits,
`creditsBalance: 18`, `creditsUsedThisMonth: 0`, Stripe
`evt_1UJMLVDJXySzNBdfMWbUrSs3` in live mode. The "108s" badge is 90 free + 18.
They spent none of it, because there was nothing they could spend it on.

## Four defects, all ours

### 1. The manual-marker submit button was off-screen — FIXED

Their viewport is 1272x554 (a 1280x720 laptop screen). The modal is capped at
`max-h-[90vh]`, and Cancel / "Mark error" sat at the bottom of its scrolling
body. Measured on a replica in headless Chrome: **"Mark error" at y=607–639,
modal content ends at 526.** The last visible control is "Replace with…", and
that is exactly the last thing they clicked before closing the modal, twice.
The button was also disabled until a box was drawn, and nothing said so.

Fix (`components/ManualMarker.tsx`): the actions moved into a footer outside
the scroll area. Verified visible at viewport heights 413, 554 and 813. The
footer names the first missing step ("Drag on the frame to draw a box around
the error.") instead of showing a greyed-out button with no reason.

### 2. mailto: reached no one, for the second user in a row — FIXED

They clicked "Email us" 10 s after their third upload, and "Help" in the menu
3½ minutes later. The 2026-09-23 user did the same. On a desktop browser with
no mail handler, a `mailto:` click does nothing.

Fix: every `mailto:help@scenefixer.com` (Header menu, job page, pricing, home
×2, DropZone, ErrorCard `contact_support`) is now a `HelpLink` that opens one
`HelpDialog`, mounted in `app/layout.tsx`. It is opened by an event rather
than owned by the link, because the Header dropdown unmounts on click.
`POST /api/support` **stores the request in Firestore `support_requests`
first**, then calls `notifyOwner`, then captures `support_request_sent`. A
signed-in user's account email is used; signed-out users must give one. A
honeypot field drops bots silently.

Tested end to end with Playwright against `next dev`, from the job-page link
through the dialog to the Firestore record with the job ID. Test documents
were deleted. The legal pages keep `mailto:business@alanany.com` on purpose.

Read requests with `node scripts/support-inbox.mjs` (`--all`, `--close ID`).

### 3. A missed cut became an "error" in the user's video — NOT FIXED, measured

Shots came out as exactly 0/10/20 s. Those are real cuts: the video is three
10 s AI clips joined. But there is also a hard cut at **frame 137 (4.57 s)**,
top of the stairs → bottom of the stairs, confirmed on extracted frames.
ContentDetector scored it **25.6 against the 27.0 threshold**. Opus then saw
shot 1 change environment within itself and reported a high-severity
atmosphere error. The `detect.py` guard from 2026-09-15 correctly marked it
unrepairable, but the user was still told their video has a problem we can't
fix. The problem was our shot detection.

**Threshold re-measured, and 27.0 stays.** All 192 job inputs were pulled
(84 unique by md5) and run through the production `detect_content_crop` +
`transcode_to_720p`. Going to 25.0 adds 25 boundaries, 21 of them unique.
**Hand-labelled from the frame on either side: 7 real, 13 false, 1 ambiguous.**
The false ones are fast motion (chameleons, a motion-blurred fight), gesturing
characters and slow drifts. 26.0 would not catch this user's cut at all.

A false boundary is the more expensive mistake. It splits a continuous shot
and invents a *repairable* error someone can pay for. A missed cut now lands
in the unrepairable guard. This agrees with the 77-video sweep on 2026-09-15.

**Better direction, not built:** a targeted second pass only inside shots
where Opus reports `shotAId == shotBId` with an environment change. Look for a
lower-threshold peak there and split at it. An Opus finding plus a local peak
is stronger evidence than either alone, and it touches only the shots already
known to be wrong.

### 4. Same file, three scores: 72 / 85 / 72 — NOT FIXED

It was the same error each time, rated high, medium, high. This is Opus

different answers on an identical file do not build trust.
