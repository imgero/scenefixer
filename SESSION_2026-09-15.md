# Session 2026-09-14/15 — the detection was always good; everything downstream of it was throwing the work away

Started from a PostHog export and a question: where are the new users coming
from, and are we actually fixing anything? It ended with the first verified
fixes this architecture has produced, and five bugs found in the path between
"Runway returned a good clip" and "the user downloads it".

The headline finding is that **detection was never the problem**. The owner's
instinct that analysis was working well was correct — all 37 findings on the
first test video were real. What was broken was the bookkeeping around them.

---

## 1. Where the users come from

25 people, 8–13 Sep. Channel by session: Direct 11 · ChatGPT 11 · Google 8. But
by *users who actually uploaded a video*:

| channel | activated users |
|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** | **5 of 8** |
| Google | 3 of 8 |
| Direct (no UTM) | **0** |

Two things to know:

- **ChatGPT is under-reported.** Three of the best users show
  `referring_domain = $direct` but carry `utm_source = chatgpt.com`. ChatGPT
  strips the referrer on some clients. Filtering PostHog on referrer alone
  credits ChatGPT traffic to "Direct".
- **"Direct" with no UTM is crawler noise** — four persons in Guangzhou inside
  60 seconds, 2 events each, zero interaction.

Every activated user was outside the US/UK: India ×3, Egypt, France, Czechia,
Ukraine, Jordan. Half on mobile.

---

## 2. The 30-second wall was the single biggest leak

`_MAX_VIDEO_MINUTES["free"] = 0.5` rejected **6 of 20 jobs** outright and hit
**4 of the 8 users who ever uploaded anything**. The rejected durations cluster
at 35.3s, 64.6s, 64.6s, 66.9s, 68.0s — people upload roughly one-minute clips
and the ceiling was under half of that. One user uploaded the *same* 64.62s file
twice, five minutes apart, and left.

Replaced with `_FREE_ANALYSIS_SECONDS` (300s) and **truncation instead of
rejection**: a longer video is analysed up to the budget and the boundary is
shown, with the rest offered. All six previously-rejected videos now scan in
full — including the 300.83s one, which lands just inside via the existing
floor-to-whole-seconds rule.

---

## 3. The score floored every honest result at zero

`100 - errors*8 - high*5` hits 0 from 13 errors up. Five of ten real jobs scored
0, 10, 13, 29, 31, and two users were shown a flat **0** on their own footage.

Replaced with weighted error **density per shot** on an exponential decay,
floored at 5. Rewards finding few errors across many shots instead of punishing
long videos for being long. Validated across three videos:

| video | shots | errors | errors/shot | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| test 1 (broken animation) | 14 | 22 | 1.57 | 29 |
| test 3 | 18 | 17 | 0.94 | 63 |
| test 2 (restaurant) | 2 | 3 | 1.50 | 61 |

---

## 4. One defect was being counted up to twelve times

Text-similarity dedupe catches the same object described twice. It cannot catch
a **systemic** defect, because each pair reports it against a different
reference and so describes a different object each time. A 14-shot animation
whose characters change clothes throughout produced **12 separate wardrobe
errors**, three of them on shot 13 alone — the same dress compared against three
different earlier shots.

`collapse_by_defect_class` groups on `(target shot, defect class, repairable)`.
The grade class merges lighting/atmosphere/other because they are one remedy.
**37 → 22 findings**, replayed offline first and then matched exactly live.

`repairable` is part of the key because the first version merged a fixable
finding into an unrepairable one and marked the whole group unfixable — silently
costing two real fixes, and committing exactly the compound ask
`project_repairability` exists to prevent.

---

## 5. Five bugs in the delivery path

All of these produce a job reporting **verified** over a video that is not
fixed. This is very likely why the historical numbers looked better than users'
experience.

1. **Every fix re-rendered the original.** Two fixes on one shot each started
   from `job["inputVideoUrl"]`, so the second carried its own change and not the
   first. Now **chained** — each fix starts from the previous one's output.
2. **`restitch` picked clips by arbitrary document order.** `fixed_clips[shot] =
   ...` in a loop, last write wins. Observed live: shot 2 had a wardrobe fix at
   14:39:53 and an atmosphere fix chained onto it at 14:44:35; document order
   kept the *wardrobe-only* clip, so the delivered video had the outfits fixed
   and the rain back, on a job reporting 3/3 verified. Now sorted by `fixedAt`,
   proved by frame comparison (0.91 vs 66.31 mean pixel difference).
3. **`scoreAfter` was computed on a different basis than `scoreBefore`** — the
   collapse ran in `run_detect` but not in `verify.py`. Any rule shaping the
   error list must land in both paths. (Same lesson as the adjacency rule on
   2026-09-09.)
4. **`verifyWarning` blamed Aleph for unfixed errors.** "Aleph introduced 19 new
   issues" was 19 errors the user chose not to fix, re-detected on the output.
5. **The fix route charged for every confirmed error while `run_fix_phase`
   attempts only 8.** Confirm 20 → pay 20 → 8 run → 12 never attempted and never
   refunded, because the refund only fires on a fix that ran and failed. Both
   sides now capped at `MAX_ERRORS_PER_FIX`, cross-referenced in each file.

## 6. `different_scene` was silently hiding broken videos

The flag returned `{"errors": [], "different_scene": true}` — all-or-nothing.
One uncertain judgement about whether a cut was deliberate discarded **every**
finding in the pair.

Measured, four runs per pair, same model and input:

```
shots 7->8   5 frames: found 3 errors on 3 of 4 runs
             3 frames: different_scene on 4 of 4 — found NOTHING
```

It flips on roughly **1 in 4** runs for pairs where two shots merely look
unalike — which is the most visually varied, most-broken footage, i.e. the users
who most need the findings.

**Fixed structurally, not by wording.** The flag now drops only grade-type
findings and keeps object-level ones — a jacket that changes colour is a
continuity error whether or not the scene changed around it. This is the same
distinction `GRADE_TYPES` already draws for non-adjacent pairs; `different_scene`
was a blunter second copy of it.

Result: shots 7→8 went from **0 findings to 2 kept on every run**, while the
genuinely clean user video `w023gby4` stayed at 0 (checked 4 runs — that user's
score of 100 was truthful). The prompt tightening did **not** reduce how often
the flag fires; the structural change did all the work.

`PROMPT_VERSION` → `v17` to invalidate 988 stale cache entries.

## 7. Model evaluation — measured, not assumed

Same prompts, same frames, same real shot-pairs, back to back.

| model | findings | per 60-pair scan | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Opus 4.8** | 19 (and 11/16 on repeat) | $5.02 | **keep** |
| Sonnet 5 | 3 — **16% recall** | $1.96 | rejected |
| Opus 5 | 16 | $6.01 | indistinguishable, ~20% dearer |
| Fable 5.1 | 8, plus 2 of 6 calls `529` | $12.01 | rejected |

Sonnet missed findings on **6 of 11 pairs** and bailed to `different_scene` more
often — it does not hold the prompt's "AI footage inconsistencies are not
intentional cuts" instruction.

**The variance control is the more important result.** Opus 4.8 run twice on
identical input gave **11 vs 16 findings — a 31% swing**, with two pairs flipping
between "no errors" and "3 real errors". Consequences: any A/B on this pipeline
needs a repeat-run control (an earlier caching A/B was discarded for lacking
one), and `scoreBefore` carries real noise. Users do not see it, because the
Firestore cache keys on keyframe URLs — a re-scan returns the identical cached
result. That determinism is accidental and load-bearing.

## 8. Economics

**Runway is prepaid and sunk (~$0 marginal). Claude is live cash on every scan,
including scans from people who never pay.** That inversion drives everything.

Measured: **$0.0836 per pair call**. Cost is driven by shot count, not length,
and saturates at `MAX_PAIRS=60` — a 64.6s fast-cut video already hits the cap, so
shortening the analysis window saves nothing on the videos that cost the most.

Across the 12 real analysed jobs: **median $1.04, mean $1.74 per scan**, worst
case ~$6.86. Only 2 of 12 hit the cap.

Fix costs (real, from today's runs): gemini 65 credits for a 5.9s shot; aleph2
140 for the same; aleph2 **784** for a chunked 29.4s shot. gemini is capped at
10s input so it can never exceed ~$1.10 per call; aleph2 is uncapped.

**A tried-and-reverted lever:** 3 keyframes per pair instead of 5 saves 38% and
**costs detections** (section 6). `PAIR_FRAMES` stays at 5. The prompt text
saying "typically 3 frames" is what is out of date, not the code.

Kept: shot-A prefix caching (~30%, provably inert — same content, cached). The
breakpoint sits after shot A and before shot B deliberately, so the prompt stays
last in the user turn and nothing the model sees changes.

## 9. What actually got fixed, end to end

**6 fixes attempted, 5 verified, 1 unverified-and-damaging, 1 vendor-blocked**,
across two videos and four defect classes.

- shot 7 atmosphere — `gemini_omni_flash`, verified
- shot 2 wardrobe — **`aleph2`**, verified
- shot 2 atmosphere — chained onto the above, verified
- smartphone removal, 5.9s — `aleph2` text-only, no bbox, verified
- smartphone removal, 29.4s — `aleph2` **chunked**, verified

**`aleph2` verified object-level work.** `project_fix_engines` records it as "the
engine that does NOT work" — but that was measured on whole-clip background
unification, where gemini won. The routing rule (objects → aleph2, whole-clip →
gemini) is sound; the note needs qualifying.

**And the counter-example:** retrying a moderation-blocked regrade on aleph2
produced *"heavily motion-blurred, low-resolution and over-processed"* output
with the dining chairs recoloured tan → orange, score 61 → 47. `choose_engine`
now **refuses an aleph2 override for whole-clip work**, because `diagnose.py`
offers users a "Try another engine" button and aleph2 is the only other engine.

**Runway content moderation blocked 2 of ~12 attempts** (~17%) on innocuous
content — a restaurant scene, and a prompt that was only "reduce the cool blue
cast". Vendor-side and not fixable here. `diagnose.py` handled it correctly.

## 10. Pricing moved to credits only

Subscriptions removed from `/pricing`; credit packs only ($10/18, $25/50,
$50/110, $100/240 — all four Stripe prices confirmed active on the live key).
Free grant 30 → **90 credits**, and `MONTHLY_SPEND_CEILING` raised with it —
at 30 it would have silently capped the new grant at a third.

Sizing, measured: one 6s fix costs ~$0.66 on gemini. At $1.50/shot that is a 56%
margin and a typical video is **$4.50–12 to fix**, not the $200 an earlier
(wrong) estimate suggested — that figure scaled per-error billing beyond the
video's own length and priced a job nobody would buy.

Breakeven needs analysis cost down: at $1.67/scan you need ~54% conversion; at
$0.40 with 3 free shots, ~24%. A 10% target is a customer-acquisition cost, not
breakeven — worth choosing deliberately.

- 3-free-shots at $1.50; corroboration (`mergedCount`) ranking for a
  "critical-only" view — severity is useless for this (test 1: 13 high of 22;
  test 2: **zero** high, so a critical-only view would show that user nothing).
- The cascade experiment: each image is currently sent ~8.6 times (60 pairs ×
  10 images vs 70 unique). Describe-once-then-compare could cut that, but
  description is lossy and needs measuring before it is trusted.
- `different_scene` still fires often; only its blast radius is fixed.
